As I was reading The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It, by W. J. Hennigan, I felt like there was more impact and interest in what was being discussed because of the occasional images that were shown. Upon opening the link to the reading we are introduced with a black background that has a red pulsating orb, which to me indicates that time is running out, it puts more seriousness into the topic being introduced, which is nuclear conflict, and it also catches readers attention. The one that had the biggest impact was the images that were used in showing what would happen if a nuclear missile were launched. As you scroll you see what it looks like if a missile were launched, you see the explosion and the blinding light of the explosion, you see everything being destroyed. These images along with the writing flow well together and make readers want to keep reading. I think color also had a big part in pointing out the importance of this topic, because the usage of dark, gloomy colors makes this seem more serious and also shows how life would look if a missile were to be launched.
I find this topic interesting, we don’t tend to talk about nuclear weapons at all because people think that the usage of nuclear weapons is something that wouldn’t happen for the next 100 years or so. This was an eye opening article, the thing that made me think the most was the mention of how one nuclear weapon being used can cause a domino effect in which different continents start to experience some negative impact even if they weren’t directly hit by the weapon. I think people, especially in the US, tend to ignore the usage of nuclear weapons because they think they are the ones who will stay safe if there were a nuclear weapon to ever be used. This thinking is completely wrong, even if your country isn’t the one getting hit by the nuclear weapon, your country will still be impacted because so many countries trade with each other so if one is hit then that trading system gets broken which impacts every other country in that system. This article did well in pointing out every problem that is happening when it comes to nuclear weapons. Not only did it bring out things that were done in the past to control the making/usage of nuclear weapons, but it also pointed out problems that were happening now. The author emphasized a lot that the problem is still really big right now, because people tend to think that problems with nuclear weapons were only a thing of the past but this clearly shows it isn’t.
Overall I really had fun reading this article, even though the topic itself isn’t one that should be considered fun, this article made it fun so that you keep reading on the seriousness of nuclear weapons. It's like a warning, the author is telling readers to keep track of what countries have a higher probability of using a nuclear weapon, and is showing readers on the impacts this can have and why it's a big deal.
